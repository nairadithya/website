Cars 2 has always been treated as the ugly duckling in the Cars franchise. I'm here to defend it. I think this film is the most logical next step you could have taken without playing it safe.

To write the first Cars film, the weird geniuses in Pixar went out to find all the different ways that cars affected our life.

The first film is feverishly devoted to vehicles, it's about (fictional) racing history rooted in real racing history. It's about settlements that get bulldozed over in the name of progress (quite literally by a bypass). Everything is derived from first principles about trying to do right by the concept of cars. The clouds are tire tracks. The bugs are cars. Everything is Cars. Even God.

Now, if we think about this, there are only three major ways that cars have manifested in media:

1.  The racing film (Your Days Of Thunder, Speed Racer, fast cars competing in high octane sports)
2.  The spy film (The Cool Car in every James Bond film, The getaway car, the car that saves the day, the Aston Martin DB5)
3.  The Fast And The Furious (I love whatever the hell they are)

If we take this premise and we have explored all that you can that sounds like a first-order effect of a world of Cars&hellip; how would you make a sequel?

A sequel needs to do two things for me to be a good sequel:

1.  It needs to be expand on the central theme of the franchise
2.  It needs to take the story forward in a new direction (no rehash, no Star Wars Episode VII)

Let's say you've made a beloved movie about anthropomorphized cars. Kids all around the world love them. What could you make that is a sequel that kids would still love while not betraying the root theme of your story?

Spies. Spies are so damn cool. The spy car is intrinsically awesome. There's so much novelty in a car chase with a car that's fitted out with all these cool contraptions to get away from people chasing them or something else. James Bond is of course the template, and they make it a point to bring out a new car with a new toy in almost every film. Even films that parody James Bond don't forget about the car. James Bond's car is as vital to James Bond as a shaken vodka martini.

If you're with me so far, If spies were to be the natural next step for your film about cars, then you'd need an evil moustache twirling villain with a political allegiance. Enter Professor Z, a vaguely Russian monocled Car that has been downtrodden all his life.
This evil moustache twirling villain is an oil baron, making faulty alternative fuel to steer public outlook away from alternative fuel to line up his pockets and also he's trying to kill the Queen Of England.

What could be more James Bond than that?

Now how do we do this without betraying the core of the story? The story is still about McQueen and Mater's friendship. It's about McQueen underestimating Mater and not respecting his friendship. McQueen is still a racer, but the genius of the framing is that he's a celebrity target for the spy plot that this film is oriented around. It's about Mater's self-actualization beyond just McQueen's friend.

The best part of all this is that the folks at Pixar COMMIT to the decision.

Michael Giacchino's score for Finn McMissile is so effortlessly cool at emulating the vibes of a James Bond esque film. The plot beats match so perfectly with a traditional spy film. This film is such a loveletter to the genre.

Given all this context, let's examine Cars 2 as a sequel:

1.  Completely new direction? YES
2.  True to the core theme of friendship? YES

This film rocks.

Cars 3 on the other hand is cowardice while still being a pretty good film.

P.S. - I am also grateful for Cars 2 introducing weaponry into the Cars franchise because the movie tie-in game is so much fun. I love shooting up my friends and racing around. It's a delightful time all around.
