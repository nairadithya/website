About a year and a half ago, [Nikhil](https://heftymouse.me) showed me [Nathan's blog post about IndiaFOSS 2024](https://polarhive.net/blog/indiafoss-2024/), we ended up going in September 2025 and it was cooler than the blog post made it out to be. 

This was about a week or so after we founded [ATLAS](https://atlasdev.club), and we had such an incredible time there. Talented and passionate people everywhere, students just like us and communities that were so aligned with everything we wanted ATLAS to be.

IndiaFOSS 2026 had such a bizarre contrast compared to last year. Over the past year, so many incredible people from different pockets of the Bengaluru tech scene assembled in one spot. Old friends from the last IndiaFOSS, friends from [IndieWebClub](https://blr.indiewebclub.org/), [Homebrew](https://homebrew.hsp-ec.xyz/) and [amFOSS](https://amfoss.in/) along with [Nithitsuki](https://nithitsuki.com/)'s assorted friends from [threadlocked](https://threadlockers.github.io/).

Another cool thing about this year is that ATLAS has grown into a solid community of people interested in tech. The last year we had about 4 or 5 people with us at IndiaFOSS. This year from a single message in our group chat, over 30 people had come in from our college alone. I'm really happy to see so much enthusiasm for the things that I love about tech.

Of all the people I got to see again, I was incredibly excited to meet [Aman](https://aman.bh/) and [Vonter](https://x.com/Vonterinon) from [Diagram Chasing](https://diagramchasing.fun). We met Aman and Vivek in the last IndiaFOSS, where we talked about their incredibly cool work around building data stories and our (at the time) [tiny college webring](https://amrita.town). My personal favourites from their already incredible list of projects are [How Bangalore Uses The Metro](https://diagramchasing.fun/2025/how-bangalore-uses-the-metro), [CBFC Watch](https://cbfc.watch/) and [Who Is My Neta?](https://diagramchasing.fun/2024/who-is-my-neta). Seeing them again was a genuine delight.

Shoutout to Abhyudhaya and the rest of the [COSH](https://cosh.nitk.ac.in/home) team for setting up the network infra and showing us the behind the scenes for it all. They pulled an all nighter to set up access points all over the convention center so that attendees could have Internet access.

The biggest ROI I had last year was writing Emacs in the "Interested in..." field on my sticker badge. It was a slightly out there choice, and it got people asking me about it. I did a two-parter this time. I wrote in Emacs on day 1 and Pantheon on day 2. The goal was that I'd attack two different groups that I was interested in getting to know. It was only after I wrote out Pantheon that Nikhil told me that people might assume I'm talking about the ElementaryOS desktop environment rather than the excellent science fiction show that I am currently obsessed with. Shoutout to [Mukesh](https://0xmukesh.github.io/) for validating me and talking about the show.

## Talks
1. [KC's talk on building runnable textbooks](https://fossunited.org/c/indiafoss/2026/cfp/8s4g5qj3oq) - The idea itself inspires me. The biggest roadblock we keep thinking about while running events is the setup time. Removing that burden so that the attendees can simply get to learning the thing they wanted to learn sounds very useful to setup.
2. [Building tech communities that thrive](https://fossunited.org/c/indiafoss/2026/cfp/42vnv417j4) - Lots of things to think about from this session, as one of the leaders in a student community myself.
3. [Modern techniques for flexibility in strongly typed functional programming](https://fossunited.org/c/indiafoss/2026/cfp/d8b9uvdntg) - This talk had so much FP fanservice, The speaker managed to hit all the FP catchphrases ("Parse don't validate", "Making invalid states unrepresentable", etc.) and I love massaging my types. Nikhil and I had a lovely conversation with Anupam afterwards. 
4. [Shaili Sanchaya](https://fossunited.org/c/indiafoss/2026/cfp/an3ot6c0v0) - One of the things I'm very interested in is preservation. There are so many infinities to capture in our time here on Earth and I have so much respect for anyone taking the time to preserve and modernize things from the past. This talk was about a pipeline for transcribing old Kannada manuscripts and building out OpenType fonts for the language.
5. [Bezier Curves in GTK](https://fossunited.org/c/indiafoss/2026/cfp/0rlqb6s6lb) - [Aman](https://www.linkedin.com/in/aman-valiyakath/) is a friend, so I couldn't not go to his talk. The talk was so worth it, it managed to straddle the line between unhinged and informative effortlessly.
6. [Seqout](https://fossunited.org/c/indiafoss/2026/cfp/f4ko2mik8q) - A talk delivered by a friend, [Mukesh](https://0xmukesh.github.io/). Always cool to see how you can adapt lessons and methods from other domains and solve problems for people that way.

The IndiaFOSS organizers did a fun thing this year with the last leg of Day 2, Communi-Con. A bunch of ten minute talks that were chosen by popular vote. I really like the idea because the talks themselves feel like they have significantly lower stakes than a talk you need to prepare for months for. I think I speak better off the cuff than when I rehearse endlessly and get all in my head about what to say.

## Booths
I didn't get a chance to visit all the booths like I did last year, but here are my personal highlights:

1. Diagram Chasing - The booth had a cool quiz and a lovely set of cards from their projects. I bought the Diagram Chaser's guide and Aman was kind enough to give me the Namma Metro stat card.
2. Bengawalk - They had a cool map of all the BMTC bus routes and the positions of each bus. I am a huge fan of their work. Their work on YouTube was one of the first things that truly radicalized me about how much better my city can be.
3. Absurd Industries -  I loved the lightsaber. One of the main things I wanted to see from [Nathan's blog post](https://polarhive.net/blog/indiafoss-2024/) that I finally got around to seeing.

Overall, I had a blast. Getting to meet people who are on your wavelength, all concentrated in one place. Seeing old friends and other cool hackers who inspire us and were inspired by us all in one place. I will remember this IndiaFOSS even more fondly than the last one, and I genuinely cannot wait for the next one, hopefully with a booth or a talk (no promises of course).

P. S. At the end of day 1, we went to Bengaluru Cafe, which was a couple kilometres away. Pretty great dosa and coffee, just don't expect to be able to sit down and eat peacefully.
